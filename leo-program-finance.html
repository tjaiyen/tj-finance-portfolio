Chapter 4

What I'd Actually Do About This

  1. Model the waiver-condition exposure explicitly, not just the deadline. Priority-status loss is a real, disclosed consequence — put a dollar/schedule figure on what losing priority status actually costs the program, and track it as its own line, not folded into the general milestone-risk narrative.
  2. Re-baseline the multi-year CapEx curve to the realized deployment rate, not the theoretical one. If capacity was never really the constraint, the OP1/OP2 plan's capex-decline assumption should be tested against a launch-constrained scenario, not a manufacturing-constrained one.
  3. Bring the ROI framework to the launch-diversification conversation before it's asked for. With two of three rocket families already disrupted this year, "what's the payback on a fourth launch provider" is a question finance should already have modeled, not one it waits to be asked.
  4. Flag the unlaunched-inventory carrying cost now, while it's a planning conversation — not later, when it's a write-down conversation. A 1,251–1,641-satellite range sitting as unlaunched inventory has a real capitalization and obsolescence-risk angle worth surfacing early.
  5. Get ahead of the D2D filing's budget ask. A regulatory front sized larger than the entire original constellation is a multi-year capital conversation that shouldn't land on someone's desk as a surprise once the FCC clock starts running.
  6. Wire the operational KPI framework to real numbers, not a slide. Two of its 54 targets already fail when checked against this page's own data — the build-rate target and the FCC compliance margin. A framework that isn't checked against reality every cycle is a wish list, not a scorecard.

This is Ownership and Bias for Action in practice — not waiting for perfect information, moving with the best available data, and being ready to say so out loud.

Glossary

Every term this page uses, in plain language. Nothing here assumes you've read a program P&L before.

BAC — Budget at Completion

What you said the whole thing would cost. The number in the approved plan.

AC — Actual Cost

Money genuinely spent to date. No judgement attached about whether it bought anything.

EV — Earned Value

The budgeted value of work actually finished. The bridge between spend and progress, and the number most reports quietly skip.

PV — Planned Value

What you should have finished by now according to the schedule.

CPI — Cost Performance Index

EV ÷ AC. Below 1.0 means every dollar bought less than a dollar of progress. As a hypothetical example, a CPI of 0.75 means 75 cents of work for every dollar spent -- a generic illustration, not this page's own CPI (see the EVM section above for the real one).

SPI — Schedule Performance Index

EV ÷ PV. Below 1.0 means behind the planned curve. It says nothing about cost.

CV / SV — Cost & Schedule Variance

The same two ideas in dollars instead of ratios. Negative is bad in both.

EAC — Estimate at Completion

What it will now cost given what's happened so far. Three versions exist because each embeds a different belief about whether the overrun repeats.

VAC — Variance at Completion

BAC minus EAC. How far over or under budget you now expect to finish.

TCPI — To-Complete Performance Index

The efficiency you'd have to hit from here to land on a target. If it comes out negative, the target is already unreachable.

OP1 / OP2

Amazon's annual planning cycle — OP1 in the autumn, OP2 as the January reset. The documents a Finance Manager is measured against.

CapEx vs. OpEx

CapEx buys things that last and is spread over years. OpEx is the cost of running this year. Moving spend between them changes reported profit without changing cash.

TCO — Total Cost of Ownership

Unit price plus everything unit price hides: tooling, qualification, yield loss, carrying cost, switching cost.

Payback & ROI

Payback is how long until an investment returns its cost. ROI is the annual return as a percentage of it. Neither prices risk on its own.

Variance drivers

Price (paid a different rate), volume (bought a different quantity), scope (bought something unplanned), timing (right money, wrong period). Only timing reverses on its own.

Pareto analysis

Ranking causes so you can see which handful explain most of the effect — the vital few against the long tail.

Capitalized inventory

Finished goods carried on the balance sheet at cost rather than expensed. Fine while they're worth what you paid; a write-down when they're not.

Priority status (FCC)

First claim on an orbit and a frequency. Losing it isn't losing the license — it's losing your place in the queue, which is close to the same thing commercially.

OISL — Optical Inter-Satellite Link

Laser terminals that let satellites talk to each other instead of routing everything via the ground. Few suppliers make them well.

D2D / SCS

Direct-to-device: satellites talking straight to ordinary phones. SCS is the FCC framework that regulates it.

Exposure tier

Probability crossed with impact, expressed as a band rather than a false-precision number. A tier admits what a percentage pretends to know.
